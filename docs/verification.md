# Game verification

Run `sh scripts/check.sh` with the pinned Godot 4.7.2 runtime. All suites use the current two-week policy campaign.

- Policy content checks cover all 150 packets, twelve rules (P15 readable code among them), the two-day blocks of additions, amendments, and retirements, keyword token boundaries, per-file ink exceptions and issue-named permits, every fault and near-miss wording of the comment and def standards (the colleague, HR's words, ghost TODOs), and the P15 readable-code boundaries the Helios payloads break. `test_records` covers the issue and build standards at their boundaries (vibes labels and lookalikes, any status, Urgent and High, Fibonacci estimates and 0 before and after the amendment, failed, flaky, and overridden builds, banned and lookalike branches and hashes), record evidence accepted and rejected (WHOLE FILE never counts), journal and save replay with record citations, revisions that fix exactly the cited record faults, Lineal and Pipeline showing the PR's own issue and build with SELECT AS EVIDENCE, and that neither app shows findings or anything still in line.
- Simulation and clock checks cover three-minute shifts, twenty-second arrivals, explicit selection, exact citations, consultation unlocks, immutable transitions, payroll, evenings, completion on the second Friday, and invalid action histories.
- Save checks exercise three independent slots, JSON round trips, backup recovery, malformed data, current tutorial progress, and the pre-release deletion of invalid or outdated saves at application start (`SaveStore.DELETE_INVALID_SAVES_ON_START`). There is no compatibility campaign.
- Story checks (`test_story`) cover payload tracking, the firing rules and Helios taking a fired coworker's seat, player firing, the six endings and the ending matrix, reject-with-no-reason grading on normal PRs and payloads, whole-team firing, and save replay of a career that fires a coworker, ships and blocks payloads, and reaches an ending.
- Interface and application checks cover source rendering, compact rule descriptions, the absence of any Slouch icon, window, badge, or ticker card, the REVIEW icon and review notification card, notification counts, pause/focus handling, window layout, tutorial arrows (orientation goes straight to REVIEW), the cold-open → orientation → morning memo → shift sequence, and Morgan's end-of-day panel: it opens at every closing (and on an evening load) with her portrait, notes, and closing words, each described evening choice advances the day, and day 10 ends with RETURN TO MAIN MENU.
- Chat content checks (`test_chat`, `test_manager`, the DM checks in `test_encounters` and `test_trees`) keep the archived Slouch conversations and Morgan's `Chat.evening` report authored and audit-free.
- Press checks cover all five daily editions, clickable stories, memo gating, and newly introduced rules without leaking audit answers.
- Desktop-window checks cover dragging, resizing, maximize/restore, clipping, and supported viewport sizes.
- Music checks (`test_music`) require the workday song to import as a looping Ogg Vorbis stream of about three minutes and at most 4 MB; check that the shift plays it alone (other scenes keep the stems), that it crossfades in and out at once, dips on pushback, resumes where it stopped within a shift, and restarts at a new BEGIN SHIFT. They load every stem and require a forward loop of exactly 16 bars at 125 BPM, continuation samples past the loop end, mono QOA, and identical lengths; check the state-to-layer mapping (intro only in the morning, menu, and end of day; warm and bleak endings); check that stem changes wait for a bar line and fade over two bars, that pause and focus loss duck behind the bus low-pass, that the MUSIC toggle and volume persist, and that SYSTEM's controls drive the manager. Under `--headless` the stems stay silent unless a test sets `Music.play_headless`, as `test_music` does to check that they start in one mix step. `tools/music/compose.py` prints each stem's peak, RMS, K-weighted loudness, and loop-seam jump when it renders.

Native and single-thread Web exports use the Compatibility renderer. Screenshots under ignored `build/` provide visual checks of the actual native controls. `sh scripts/run.sh --script res://tools/capture.gd -- <dir>` renders the menu, cold open, orientation (the arrow on REVIEW, then the file dropdown), morning reader, the desktop mid-shift, Review, standards, a stamped review, Morgan's end-of-day panel (day 1, a later day, and the final RETURN TO MAIN MENU), an author pushing back (INSIST/WITHDRAW), an author revising at the desk (typing, then v2), pause, a Helios payload on the desk with its author pleading, a reason-free rejection, Morgan announcing a coworker's firing, and the ending cinematics (a matrix ending and a player firing) to PNGs. `sh scripts/run.sh --headless --script res://tools/encounter_flowchart.gd -- <out.html>` exports the encounter flow charts to a self-contained HTML page. Displayed source is never executed. Windows packaging, notarized distribution, sound effects, and comprehensive screen-reader support remain outside this milestone.
